# Platform Review Synthesis

Status: post-publication review synthesis  
Applies to: Sutton SignWriting Platform v1.0.0  
Text status: published platform and series artifacts unchanged

## Purpose

This file records platform-level lessons from post-publication review without revising the DOI-bearing v1.0.0 artifacts.

It does not reproduce individual reviews, identify reviewers, or archive private correspondence. It preserves synthesized feedback that affects the Sutton SignWriting Platform as a whole: cross-series themes, routing decisions, recurring risks, and future-use notes.

## Review Handling

Review synthesis preserves critical engagement without silently changing stable citation records.

Feedback is routed to the smallest public layer it actually concerns:

- platform-wide issues belong in the platform record
- whole-series issues belong in the relevant series repository
- entry-specific issues belong near the entry they address
- future-edition or standalone-article ideas should be marked as future planning, not as corrections to v1.0.0

The review posture is public, sober, and non-defensive. A review note should clarify what review has shown, what belongs elsewhere, what should inform future work, and where the platform boundary should remain intact.

## Review And Rewrite Notes

Two note types are useful across the platform and companion series repositories.

Use `review.md` for synthesized critique:

- what review clarified
- what is minor
- what is handled elsewhere
- what is substantive
- what editorial boundary decision follows

Use `rewrite.md` only when review suggests a major alternate version of a document, such as a second edition, standalone article, response paper, or new structure.

A rewrite note is not a correction to the published v1.0.0 artifact. It is future planning. It should state that the published text remains unchanged and identify whether the proposed rewrite is exploratory, deferred, or adopted for future work.

## Overall Assessment

Early review reinforced the value of the eight-series architecture.

Signed-language writing, evidence, literacy, technical text modeling, Unicode compatibility, critical review, infrastructure, and stewardship are different layers of the full problem. The platform should not make each document carry all of them.

The central review lesson is:

> Do not ask each essay to carry the whole platform. Ask whether it defines its layer clearly, makes the right handoffs, and avoids collapsing writing, evidence, encoding, literacy, infrastructure, standards, and deployment into one another.

This is especially important for *Writing and Representation in Sutton SignWriting*. That series is the theory-of-the-object lane: it explains what kind of written object the rest of the platform preserves, studies, reviews, cites, encodes, renders, and supports.

That object is not merely a symbol inventory, image, font, Unicode block, notation, dictionary entry, video substitute, data format, or literacy outcome. It is authored signed writing as durable text.

## Platform Adequacy

Review notes should distinguish platform adequacy from standalone article adequacy.

A platform entry succeeds when it defines its layer, makes the right handoffs, and avoids collapsing neighboring claims. It may still need more examples, citations, literature review, adversarial objections, methods, or evidence if converted into a standalone academic article.

That distinction prevents modularity from being mistaken for omission.

The other series carry neighboring burdens:

- *Foundations* gives public orientation and conceptual entry.
- *Evidence and Adoption* supplies documented public use, country anchors, adoption boundaries, and evidence claims.
- *Signed Language Writing Critical Review Series* tests literature and standards through the layer-based framework.
- *Formal SignWriting* gives the technical model that preserves the written object.
- *Language and Literacy Research* turns platform claims into research questions about reading, learning, fluency, pathways, and outcomes.
- *Infrastructure and Program* handles stewardship, host structure, collections, permissions, maintenance, and program design.
- *Unicode and SignWriting* handles standards-facing and compatibility issues without letting Unicode become the whole theory of writing.

## Minor Refinements

Review identified presentation improvements that may help future public explanation without changing the v1.0.0 record.

- Some concepts would benefit from short comparison language for readers approaching SignWriting through familiar categories such as alphabetic writing, diacritics, character blocks, notation, or Unicode.
- Some entries use a clipped, programmatic style. That style supports architectural navigation, while future journal-facing versions may use more continuous academic prose.
- Some bridge language could be more explicit when readers move from Foundations to Writing and Representation, or from Writing and Representation to Formal SignWriting.

These are reception and presentation issues rather than platform-level defects.

## Addressed Elsewhere

Several review themes are valid but belong in specific series rather than in every document.

- Questions about adoption scale, country evidence, institutional anchors, and resistance belong primarily in *Sutton SignWriting Evidence and Adoption*.
- Questions about reading fluency, cognitive processing, literacy development, and study design belong primarily in *Sutton SignWriting Language and Literacy Research*.
- Questions about signboxes, coordinates, grammar, search, rendering, styling, and implementation belong primarily in *Formal SignWriting*.
- Questions about official Unicode SignWriting, FSW, SWU, compatibility, Wikimedia, and facial-orthography boundary work belong primarily in *Unicode and SignWriting* and the relevant critical review entry.
- Questions about long-term maintenance, hosting, international participation, AI/data governance, and funding belong primarily in *Sutton SignWriting Infrastructure and Program*.
- Questions about external scholarship, standards, notation systems, and full-path adequacy belong primarily in the *Signed Language Writing Critical Review Series*.

The platform should continue to make these routing decisions explicit.

## Substantive Issue

The main platform-level risk is flattening.

Readers may try to collapse Sutton SignWriting into a familiar adjacent category:

- visual notation
- pictorial drawing
- featural script
- character inventory
- font or rendering system
- Unicode block
- dataset or dictionary infrastructure
- classroom aid
- signed-language video substitute

The platform response is layer discipline. Sutton SignWriting is a human writing system. Formal SignWriting is a technical text model. FSW and SWU are encoding strategies. Fonts and SVG belong to rendering. Evidence, literacy, standards, deployment, and stewardship require their own arguments.

The platform should continue to protect that architecture in future explanations.

## Recurring Reception Patterns

Post-publication discussion showed several predictable first-pass misreadings.

These are not signs that readers are careless. They are signs that the platform crosses categories that are usually kept separate.

Recurring patterns include:

- treating the platform as advocacy because it is author-led and DOI-archived rather than a conventional peer-reviewed article
- treating low visibility in English-language literature as evidence that SignWriting evidence is weak or absent
- treating video, gloss, transcription, datasets, AI tools, or Unicode records as if they answer the same problem as durable written signed-language text
- treating documented use as if it were a claim of universal adoption
- treating Formal SignWriting, FSW, SWU, Unicode, fonts, or rendering as if they were the human writing system itself
- treating the critical review framework as a defense of one system rather than as an interested but inspectable layer discipline
- treating alphabetic signed-writing proposals as either simply wrong or obviously superior, rather than as bridge or transfer hypotheses that still require full-path evidence

The best platform response is not to add defensive caveats to every document.

The better response is a short platform-level framing guide that sets the aperture before readers enter the series.

That guide now lives in [how-to-read-the-platform.md](./how-to-read-the-platform.md).

A companion review document applies the same layer discipline to recurring concerns, distinguishing valid critique from misrouted critique and naming open work. That document now lives in [layer-aware-evaluation.md](./layer-aware-evaluation.md).
