# Sutton SignWriting Platform

## Complete Eight-Series Release

The Sutton SignWriting Platform is the umbrella record for the first archival release of eight coordinated publication series on signed-language writing, evidence, writing-system theory, Formal SignWriting, literacy research, critical review, infrastructure, and Unicode-facing work.

The central problem addressed by this release is signed-language literacy as durable written text. It asks what is needed for signed languages to be read, written, revised, searched, cited, taught, archived, and revisited as text, not only recorded, annotated, represented, or analyzed.

The central claim of the platform release is that signed languages can have durable written infrastructure for that problem: authored texts, searchable encodings, rendering systems, educational use, dictionaries, archives, software, and standards-facing records.

Here, "platform" means a coordinated archival publication and infrastructure record. It does not mean a single software application, a claim of community authority, or a ninth content series.

## Evaluation Frame

This release evaluates systems relative to the problem they claim to solve. For signed-language literacy as durable written text, a system must support a writable, readable, revisitable text pathway. Systems built for notation, linguistic analysis, corpus annotation, video access, semantic representation, synthesis, datasets, or AI infrastructure may be valuable for their own purposes, but they should not be treated as solutions to the literacy problem unless they provide that text pathway.

This is a problem-specific adequacy claim, not a global ranking of all signed-language tools. The release therefore distinguishes writing-system claims from representation claims, technical encoding claims, evidence claims, standards claims, dataset claims, and deployment claims.

## Author Position and Evidence

This release is authored by Steve Slevinski as a technical, documentary, and theoretical synthesis of Sutton SignWriting infrastructure for signed-language writing and literacy. It is not an ethnographic study, an outcome study, or a claim to measure community preference.

Its evidence base includes decades of public signed-text authorship, educational use, dictionary work, software use, and institutional activity involving Deaf signers, signing communities, educators, researchers, and technical stewards. The platform's central claim is that the writing infrastructure has been tested and shaped through documented public use, as described in the evidence and adoption series.

This platform record does not by itself provide community review, deployment approval, or permission for dataset or AI use. Those responsibilities belong to the actors and institutions working at those layers.
